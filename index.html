Abstract

This document collects the professional and scholarly record of an software engineering & AI consultant working across three domains: applied artificial intelligence, defense and public-sector modernization, and health equity. The work spans four years — building mission-critical software for federal and state government, carrying field research into policy, and representing more than sixty-seven thousand students on the governing board of a major public university.

A single question recurs across it: how technical systems can be built to serve the people least well served by the systems they replace. That question runs from a offender-management modernization to a maternal-health application, from a national-security analysis of foreign uses of artificial intelligence1 to a replatforming effort for the Navy Chaplain Corps. What follows is organized as a paper: experience and governance, built products, research and scholarship, and the interests that inform them.

1.1SWE Consultant — Department of the Navy, Chaplain Corps

Software Engineer · Religious Ministry Assessment Tool (RMAT) — September 2025 – Present · Defense, Security & Justice

Software engineer on the build and replatforming of the Religious Ministry Assessment Tool (RMAT), the mission system that supports Navy warfighter spiritual readiness. I lead technical working sessions with stakeholders at the Pentagon, translating operational needs into platform requirements and engineering decisions.

I designed the criteria and subcriteria framework used to evaluate replatforming options from the ground up, then led the comparative analysis across ServiceNow, Qualtrics, Power Apps, and Qlik against technical, operational, and federal cybersecurity requirements (DoD, CANES, Falcon Speed, IL4). To keep the recommendation grounded in reality, I ran SME interviews and discovery so that end-user needs and compliance standards were embedded in the design rather than bolted on. I authored the operational-availability report for the current ServiceNow RMAT and helped manage bug-resolution reporting to drive continuous usability improvement.

On the engineering side I scoped secure migration approaches and phase planning for Chaplain Corps data — backup, cleansing, and structured transition — while ensuring every candidate solution satisfied CAC authentication, Impact Level 4, and shipboard/offline operational needs across classified networks.

Key Result A transparent, criteria-driven assessment methodology now positions the Chaplain Corps for a secure, scalable, user-centered analytics solution supporting more than 2,500 chaplains.

Three products built end to end. Each is presented as a short case study — the problem, the approach, and what came of it.

2.1BabySafeHealth

babysafehealth.com

React Native · Maternal & Infant Health · Venture-backed

Problem. New Jersey has among the widest racial gaps in maternal and infant mortality in the country, and the mothers most at risk are the least likely to have information, monitoring, and trusted providers within reach.

Approach. An all-in-one companion for expecting mothers, built in React Native and shaped by the NSF I-Corps customer-discovery method and 25+ stakeholder interviews. It pairs a symptom tracker for blood pressure, blood sugar, and weight with a searchable, reviewed directory of maternal providers and a community home feed — with week-by-week education and multilingual editions on the roadmap.

Outcome. Won the $25,000 TechUnited Better Wellness Challenge (2021), pitched at the Propelify Innovation Festival to executives from RWJBarnabas Health, Colgate-Palmolive, and Labcorp. Selected for the Clinton Global Initiative University '22 accelerator and named a semi-finalist in the AWS U.S. University Start-Up Competition.

2.2Justice Health

fauzanamjad.github.io/JusticeHealth

Field Research · Correctional Healthcare · Policy

Problem. Incarcerated people — often arriving with untreated mental illness and substance-use disorders — depend on correctional systems for care, yet only 4 of 50 states deliver that care through a university. The recurring failure is not a single lapse but one of cadence and continuity.

Approach. The first program of its kind in the country, run through Rutgers Honors College and University Correctional Healthcare. Six undergraduate researchers — I led the team — spent a summer alongside clinicians inside New Jersey's correctional system, conducting fieldwork across five domains: sleep and environment, pharmaceutical access and medication cycling, mental-health screening and continuity, workforce development and reentry, and women's health and menstrual-product access.

Outcome. A comprehensive white paper of findings and policy recommendations. Among them: 72% of the incarcerated women studied were classified as poor sleepers, and facilities provided only two weeks of medication at release — a gap that undoes months of in-facility treatment.

2.3Acetlinx

fauzanamjad.github.io/acetlinx.html

Open Educational Resource · CS Education · Access

Problem. AP Computer Science A is a gateway to college credit and a computing career, but strong preparation is unevenly distributed — and women and minority students are underrepresented at the door.

Approach. A free, self-study video curriculum covering introductory computer science in Java, published openly on YouTube so any student can prepare for the AP Computer Science A exam without cost or gatekeeping.

Outcome. An openly accessible course that lowers the cost and time barrier to college credit and widens the on-ramp into computer science for students who would otherwise be left out.

3.1Health Equity & Clinical Research

Roles across four institutions, one recurring subject.

Cancer Institute of NJ
Research Assistant (2024). Investigated disparities in breast-cancer mortality across underserved populations using NIH All of Us data — modeling how socioeconomic status, geography, and discrimination (via Everyday Discrimination Scale scores) shape outcomes, and applying machine learning to flag high-risk groups, all under federal data-governance and patient-privacy controls.
RWJ Women's Health
Research Intern, Women's Health Institute, Robert Wood Johnson Medical School (2021–2024). Conducted 25+ semi-structured stakeholder interviews to reduce infant mortality in New Jersey and applied the Lean Startup method to design a maternal-health application, alongside maternal-education outreach for underserved communities.
Correctional Health
Research Contractor & Project Lead, University Correctional Healthcare / NJ Department of Corrections (2022). Led a team of interns and authored a literature review on sleep deprivation in correctional facilities — the basis of the Justice Health white paper.
Merck & Co.
Data Analytics & Research Contractor (2021). Analyzed survey data on vaccine knowledge and hesitancy in minority communities using Python and R, examining faith-based influence, side-effect concern, and provider communication across income bands, and recommending culturally-informed public-health strategies.
Vicario Lab
AI Research Assistant, Rutgers Psychology (2021–2024). Built and trained TensorFlow models to label zebra-finch behavior in response to auditory stimuli, studying the neural mechanisms of auditory discrimination and sensorimotor learning.
